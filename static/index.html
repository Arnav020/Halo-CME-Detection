    02 — Methodology

    From raw ion spectra to a calibrated decision.

    The pipeline is deliberately shallow: four interpretable ratios computed over a three-day window, scored by three complementary learners.

    1. 01

      Acquisition

      Aditya-L1 SWIS Level-2 plasma moments: Np, Vp, Tp and Nα at five-minute cadence.

    2. 02

      Labelling

      Halo events from the CACTUS LASCO catalogue define T0; windows span T−1 d to T+2 d. Quiet periods sampled as negatives.

    3. 03

      Feature engineering

      Composition, thermodynamic and turbulence ratios computed row-wise, then averaged over the window.

    4. 04

      Inference

      Soft-voting ensemble outputs a probability; a 0.45 threshold is tuned to eliminate missed CMEs.

    Nα / Np

    Alpha–proton ratio

    CME ejecta carries an enhanced helium abundance relative to the ambient wind. A sustained rise is one of the strongest in-situ signatures.

    σ(Vp)15 min

    Speed variability

    Shock-driven turbulence produces short-scale speed fluctuations. A centred three-sample rolling standard deviation captures it.

    (Nα/Np) / σ(Vp)

    Alpha over Vp std

    Combines composition and coherence: high values isolate alpha-rich, low-turbulence ejecta cores behind the sheath.

    Nα / Tp

    Alpha–temperature ratio

    Magnetic clouds are anomalously cool for their speed. Relating alpha density to proton temperature exposes that thermodynamic signature.

    Why in-situ detection

    Halo CMEs are the most geo-effective solar events: they disturb satellite operations, degrade GNSS accuracy, drive geomagnetic storms and stress power infrastructure. Conventional detection depends on coronagraph imagery (LASCO, CACTUS) with ground processing delays.

    Scoring plasma moments directly makes detection lightweight enough for on-board execution and removes the imaging dependency, shortening the warning chain.
